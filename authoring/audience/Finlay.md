# Audience: Stories for Finlay

## Target Audience: Finlay (2 months old)

At 2 months, Finlay cannot follow narrative. A "story for Finlay" is really a sensory read-aloud experience — the parent's voice, rhythm,
and the images in front of him are the whole point.

## Text Guidelines

* **Keep it extremely short.** One or two sentences per page is ideal. He has no attention span for plot.
* **Rhythm and repetition over narrative.** Simple rhyme, repeated phrases, or a steady beat help hold his attention via sound alone.
* **Address him directly.** Sentences like *"Can you see the big red rocket, Finlay?"* work well — the parent says his name, which he is
  beginning to recognise.
* **Vocabulary doesn't matter** — rich or simple, he responds to the sound and cadence of the words, not their meaning. Prefer words that
  are fun to say aloud: round vowels, soft consonants, bouncy rhythm.

## Image Guidelines

* **High contrast is essential.** At 2 months, vision is still developing — Finlay sees high-contrast edges most clearly. Black-and-white or
  bold primary colours on a plain background are ideal.
* **Simple, large shapes.** Single subject per image where possible. No busy backgrounds. Big round faces are especially engaging.
* **Faces over scenery.** Babies this age are drawn to faces. Illustrations that feature a clear, simple face (character, animal, or
  abstract face-like shape) will hold his attention longer than landscape scenes.
* When generating image prompts for a Finlay-primary story, specify: *high contrast, bold outlines, simple background, large central
  subject, face visible*.

## If Finlay is a Co-Audience (story primarily for Ruaridh)

* No changes to story structure needed — Finlay simply comes along for the voice and movement.
* Include one or two moments in the text where the parent can bounce, tap, or touch Finlay in sync with the story (e.g., a repeated sound or
  a "boop" moment). This is optional but nice.
