# The Great Garden SOS

*A daytime adventure for Ruaridh and Finlay*

---

**CRACKLE. CRACKLE. BEEP-BEEP-BEEP.**

Something was coming from Dad's shed.

<!-- pagebreak -->

![[Illustration: Dad at his radio set, family arrives at shed door]](./images/spread-1.png)

Dad was hunched over his radio set — a tangle of wires and dials, with a big silver antenna poking out the window like a giraffe's neck.

"Someone — or *something* — is sending a signal from the garden," Dad said, eyes going wide and delighted. "We need to investigate."

Mum came round the corner with Finlay on her hip. **"What kind of signal?"** she asked, already making a Plan Face. Ruaridh ran up behind her, boots on the wrong feet, going fast regardless. Max skidded through the shed door with a **WOOF** that meant *I have been waiting for this all morning*.

<!-- pagebreak -->

Max lifted his black nose.

He sniffed left. He sniffed right. His floppy ears went **up**.

Then he trotted — straight into the garden.

<!-- pagebreak -->

![[Illustration: Max leads the family through the vegetable patch]](./images/spread-2.png)

The garden was long and full of things. Raised beds of tomatoes, trellises with runner beans climbing in spirals, a wonky bird table, and rhubarb — enormous, like an elephant had left its ears there.

"Follow Max," Dad said. "He knows."

Mum settled Finlay on a blanket in the sun, propped with cushions so he could watch the whole expedition. Finlay kicked his feet. He was clearly delighted.

Ruaridh marched after Max, chest out, muddy already, on the case.

<!-- pagebreak -->

Max stopped near the rhubarb and sat down very properly.

He looked at Ruaridh. He looked at the rhubarb. He looked at Ruaridh again.

**Ruff.**

<!-- pagebreak -->

![[Illustration: Ruaridh kneels under the rhubarb, four hedgehog shapes visible]](./images/spread-3.png)

Ruaridh knelt in the soft earth and peered under the enormous leaves.

There, in the dim green shade, was a terracotta pot lying on its side — and poking out around it were small, round, **spiky** shapes.

"Dad," Ruaridh said, in his most serious voice. "There are… *prickly potatoes*."

Dad crouched beside him, laughing softly. "Not potatoes. Count them — how many can you see?"

Ruaridh pointed at each one so the numbers would be sure. "**One… two… three… four!** Four hedgehogs, Dad!"

"Four *baby* hedgehogs," Dad said. "And look — what letter is on that label stuck in the soil?"

The label was old and white, pressed into the earth beside the pot. The letter on it was tall and proud, with two bumps on top.

"**H**," said Ruaridh. "**H** for hedgehog!"

<!-- pagebreak -->

But one small hedgehog kept bumping its nose against the pot.

Round and round. Bump, bump, bump. It couldn't find the way out.

<!-- pagebreak -->

![[Illustration: Mum tilts the pot gently, team in position, hedgehog about to escape]](./images/spread-4.png)

"They huddle inside to sleep," Mum said, with her doctor's calm. "The pot fell over and the littlest one is confused about the exit. We just need to make the opening easier to find."

"Can we fix it?" Ruaridh asked.

"That," said Mum, "is *exactly* the right question."

She gave everyone their job, like a proper plan. Dad held back the giant rhubarb leaves — **heave!** Ruaridh was very still and very quiet, which was the most important job of all. Max sat just behind them, tail going **wag-wag-wag** in a steady, confident way that meant *you've all got this*.

Mum tilted the pot — gently, gently — until its opening faced a deep pile of leaves beside the fence. A perfect, sheltered, cosy new home.

<!-- pagebreak -->

The littlest hedgehog twitched its nose.

Then it scurried — **quick as a hiccup** — straight into the leaf pile. The others followed one after another: rustle, rustle, rustle, rustle.

From his cushion throne on the grass, Finlay went *aaah!* and waved both fists at the sky.

<!-- pagebreak -->

![[Illustration: Ruaridh muddy-kneed and grinning, whole family together in the sunny garden]](./images/spread-5.png)

Ruaridh stood up slowly, muddy at the knees, grinning from ear to ear. "We did the job," he said.

"*You* did the job," Dad said. "You counted. You read the letter. You were still when it mattered."

Mum squeezed his shoulders. "Those hedgehogs are safe because of you."

Max leaned his whole warm body against Ruaridh's legs. That was Max for: *well done, I was also very helpful.*

Ruaridh went and sat beside Finlay on the blanket and held his little brother's hand. He explained the whole expedition — the radio signal, the vegetable patch, the hedgehogs, the letter H — in great detail. Finlay listened with enormous, blinking, admiring eyes.

It was, Ruaridh decided, exactly the kind of Saturday a garden is made for.

*The end.*
